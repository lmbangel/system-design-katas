Reading list

Eight books that complete the picture.

This guide teaches the breadth; these books teach the depth. If you only read one, make it DDIA; for data platforms, add Fundamentals of Data Engineering. If you're targeting payments or logistics roles, pair them with the applied designs.

Books8
Start withDDIA
In the study planWeeks 1–3

Systems and software design

The depth behind pillars 01–07 and the applied designs.

Must read

Designing Data-Intensive Applications

Martin Kleppmann & Chris Riccomini · 2nd edition

The single most recommended book for system design interviews. Replication, partitioning, consistency, batch vs stream — the theory behind every page in this guide.

Covers
01 · Distributed systems, 02 · Data & storage, 05 · Reliability & observability, 06 · Performance & scale
In the study plan
Week 1 weekend: replication, partitioning and transactions — chapters 6–8 in the 2nd edition (5–7 in the 1st). The checklist asks for chapters 6–10 (5–9 in the 1st).
Deep dive

Database Internals

Alex Petrov

B-trees, LSM trees, WAL, MVCC, storage engine internals. When an interviewer asks “why Postgres over Cassandra”, this gives you the engine-level reasoning.

Covers
02 · Data & storage, in depth
In the study plan
Week 2 weekend: the chapters on B-trees and LSM trees.
Accessible

Understanding Distributed Systems

Roberto Vitillo · 2nd edition

A gentler entry point than DDIA. Consensus, failure detection, replication — good for reinforcing pillar 01 before going deeper.

Covers
01 · Distributed systems, as reinforcement
In the study plan
Not scheduled. Read it before DDIA if pillar 01 felt heavy.
Thinking

A Philosophy of Software Design

John Ousterhout

Not about systems — about complexity, abstractions and API surfaces. Deep modules, information hiding, tactical vs strategic programming. The senior mindset book.

Covers
Code design and senior thinking, rather than one pillar
In the study plan
Week 3 weekend. It is short enough to finish in one.

Data platforms

The depth behind pillar 08 and Applied C: one book for the whole lifecycle, then one each for streaming, observability and governance.

Fundamentals of Data EngineeringReis · Housley
Must read

Fundamentals of Data Engineering

Joe Reis & Matt Housley

The DDIA of data platforms: the whole lifecycle from source systems and ingestion to storage, transformation and serving, with security, data management, orchestration and DataOps as the “undercurrents” beneath every stage. Tool-agnostic and decision-first, which is exactly how architect interviews ask about data.

Covers
08 · Data platform architecture, all thirteen concepts; Applied C · Lakehouse platform
In the study plan
Roadmap, Sprint 3 · Data platform architecture (weeks of 19 and 26 April). Read it before the lakehouse capstone.
Streaming SystemsAkidau · Chernyak · Lax
Deep dive

Streaming Systems

Tyler Akidau, Slava Chernyak & Reuven Lax

Written by the people behind Google Dataflow and Apache Beam. Event time vs processing time, windows, watermarks, triggers and late data, exactly-once processing, and the duality of streams and tables. The first chapters alone change how you answer “batch or stream?”.

Covers
08 §11 · Stream processing, 01 · Exactly-once is a lie
In the study plan
Roadmap, Sprint 3 · Data platform architecture. Chapters 1–3 (the what, where, when and how of streaming, and watermarks) first; the rest when a stream is the answer.
Data Quality FundamentalsMoses · Gavish · Vorwerck
Practical

Data Quality Fundamentals

Barr Moses, Lior Gavish & Molly Vorwerck

Where the five pillars of data observability (freshness, volume, schema, distribution, lineage) come from. Covers testing vs monitoring, anomaly detection, incident management for data, and building a culture where someone owns each dataset.

Covers
08 §12 · Data observability, 08 §9 · Correctness
In the study plan
Roadmap, Sprint 3 · Data platform architecture, alongside the observability proof for the tier-1 tables.
Data Governance: The Definitive GuideEryurek et al.
Reference

Data Governance: The Definitive Guide

Evren Eryurek, Uri Gilad, Valliappa Lakshmanan, Anita Kibunguchy-Grant & Jessi Ashdown

People, process and tools for governing data at scale: classification and cataloguing, access control and masking, data quality ownership, lineage, auditing, and regulation-driven retention and deletion. Read it for the operating model, not the vendor examples.

Covers
08 §13 · Governance, 07 · Least privilege, Applied C · Security and governance
In the study plan
Roadmap, Sprint 2 · Storage and databases (week of 4 January), where masking, row-level security and POPIA are the topic.
